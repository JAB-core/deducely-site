Start here

Three rules. That's the whole game.

A sudoku is a grid split into rows, columns and boxes. Some cells start filled in. You fill the rest so that no digit ever repeats where it shouldn't.

Every row

holds 1 to 9, each once.

Every column

holds 1 to 9, each once.

Every 3x3 box

holds 1 to 9, each once.

No maths, no guessing. Every digit you place is one you can prove. If you're guessing, there's a clue you haven't spotted yet.

Variant

Classic

The original 9x9. The other variants all build on it.

How it plays

Fill the 9x9 grid so every row, column and 3x3 box holds 1 to 9 once. Easy puzzles give you lots of starting digits. Expert puzzles give you very few.

Where to start

Pick a digit that's already on the board a lot. Go box by box and ask where that digit can go. Rows and columns that already have it rule cells out.

Variant

X-Sudoku

Classic, plus the two long diagonals.

What's different

Both corner-to-corner diagonals must also hold 1 to 9 once each. That's two extra groups to check, and two extra ways to find a digit.

How to play it

Use every Classic trick, then scan the diagonals the same way you scan a row. The centre cell sits on both diagonals, so it has to fit five groups at once.

Variant

Killer

No starting digits at all. Just cages and sums.

What's different

The board starts empty. Dashed outlines, called cages, group cells together, and the small number in the corner is what those cells add up to. Digits can't repeat inside a cage. Rows, columns and boxes still need 1 to 9 once each.

How to play it

Start with cages that can only be made one way. Pencil those digits in, then let the normal rules do the rest. Each cage is tinted so you can tell neighbours apart.

Variant

Flurry (Sudoku Mine)

No digits at all. Find the hidden snowflakes. Based on Sudoku Mine.

What's different

You don't place numbers. You find 27 snowflakes, exactly 3 in every row, column and 3x3 box. A number tells you how many of the 8 cells around it hold a snowflake. Numbered cells never hold one.

How to play it

Mark the cells you know are safe with a dot and the ones you know hide a snowflake with a flake. Look for numbers whose open cells must all be snowflakes, and rows or boxes that already have their 3.

Variant

Samurai

Five Classic grids, joined at the corners.

What's different

Four grids sit at the corners and one in the middle. The middle grid shares one 3x3 box with each corner grid. Every grid follows the Classic rules, so the shared boxes have to work for both grids at once.

How to play it

Start in the shaded shared boxes. They get help from two grids, so they fill first.

Pencil marks

How Deuce uses pencil marks

What they are

Small notes in a cell listing the digits it could still be. They don't count as answers, so they never cost you time or mistakes.

How to use them

Write marks small in the corner of a cell, on paper or in your app's pencil mode. Each time you place a digit, cross it off the marks in the same row, column and box.

Tricks Deuce uses

Four habits that solve most puzzles

137

Pencil marks

Jot the digits a cell could still be, one box or row at a time. When a cell is down to one mark, place it. Watch Deuce do it.

Only one digit fits here

Look at one empty cell. Cross off every digit already in its row, column and box. If one digit is left, that's the answer.

Only one place for this digit

Look at one box, row or column and one digit it's missing. If every other spot is blocked, the digit goes in the last one.

Pairs

Two cells in a row that can only be the same two digits, say 4 and 8, use up the 4 and the 8. Cross those digits off everywhere else in that row.
